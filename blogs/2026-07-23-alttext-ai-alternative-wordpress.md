## What AltText.ai does well

AltText.ai is the established player in this category — around 97,000 sites use it, and that scale shows up in the product. It integrates almost everywhere: WordPress, Shopify, WooCommerce, Magento, BigCommerce, Contentful, plus Chrome and Firefox extensions and a developer API. It supports 130+ languages, handles AVIF and SVG files, and its pricing is transparent and cheap to start — plans open at $5/month, and credits roll over instead of expiring.

If you manage images across several platforms, or you need one alt text engine that works the same way in Shopify and WordPress and a headless CMS, AltText.ai is hard to beat. That breadth is its real advantage, and no honest comparison should pretend otherwise.

Two things are worth knowing before you commit, though. First, the free allowance is 25 credits, one time — not an ongoing monthly free tier. Once those are gone, every image is metered. Second, the WordPress plugin has had some recent stability complaints on its support forum (site-crash reports tied to a couple of early-2026 releases). Neither is a dealbreaker, but both are the kind of thing you'd want to know going in.

## Where OpptiAI Alt Text takes a different approach

OpptiAI Alt Text is a much younger, independently-built plugin — fewer than 10 active installs at the time of writing, and I'd rather tell you that than have you find out and feel misled. It doesn't have AltText.ai's integration surface or its track record. What it has is a different design philosophy, built around three deliberate choices.

### A review step before anything goes live

This is the core difference. Most alt text tools generate a description and write it straight to the image. OpptiAI's workflow is scan → generate → **review and score** → approve → save. Nothing is written to your live site until you've looked at it. For agency work and accessibility-serious sites, that matters: AI alt text is usually good, but "usually" isn't good enough when a wrong or off-brand description is now public on a client's product page. A human approval gate turns "trust the AI" into "check, then trust."

### A genuine free tier, and no account to try it

OpptiAI gives you 10 generations before you even create an account, then 15 per month free after that. You can judge the output on your own images before committing to anything. Compared with 25 one-time credits, that's a lower-risk way to find out whether a tool actually works for your content.

### WooCommerce included, and it stays inside WordPress

Product-image optimisation — featured images and gallery images — is part of the free workflow, not a paid add-on. And the whole thing runs inside wp-admin, so there's no separate web console to learn. You scan, review, and approve from the same dashboard where you manage everything else.

## A quick way to test either one fairly

Don't decide on features — decide on output, using your own worst images. Here's a five-minute test that works for any alt text tool:

1. Pick 10 images that represent your real content — a couple of product shots, a chart or infographic, a decorative background, a photo with text in it. The awkward ones, not the easy ones.
2. Run each tool's free allowance over those 10 images.
3. Read the descriptions as if you were a screen-reader user: is each one specific and useful, or generic filler like "a group of people"? Does it correctly handle the image with text in it? Does it mark the decorative one as decorative (empty `alt=""`) rather than describing wallpaper?
4. Check how much editing each result needs before you'd put it live. That edit time, multiplied across your whole library, is the real cost — not the sticker price.
5. Note whether the tool made you approve changes or just wrote them. If it wrote them straight to your site, go confirm nothing weird landed on a live page.

Whichever tool needs the least correction on your hardest images is the one that'll actually save you time. For more on getting a whole library done once you've chosen, see [how to bulk add alt text to WordPress images](2026-07-23-how-to-bulk-add-alt-text-to-wordpress-images.md).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there a free alternative to AltText.ai for WordPress?
Yes. Several WordPress plugins offer free tiers, including OpptiAI Alt Text (15 generations/month, plus 10 before you make an account). AltText.ai itself gives 25 one-time free credits. The free allowances differ in an important way: some are one-off, others refresh monthly, so check whether the free credits renew before you judge value. For an ongoing free workflow, a monthly-refreshing tier will serve you better than a one-time grant.

### What's the difference between AltText.ai and OpptiAI Alt Text?
AltText.ai is an established, cross-platform alt text service with wide integrations (WordPress, Shopify, Magento, API), 130+ languages, and paid plans from $5/month. OpptiAI Alt Text is a newer WordPress-only plugin with a free monthly tier and a built-in review-and-approve step, so no AI-generated description goes live until you've checked it. Pick AltText.ai for breadth and scale; pick OpptiAI if you want a free, WordPress-native tool with a human quality gate.

### Do I need an API key to generate alt text in WordPress?
It depends on the tool. Some plugins ask you to bring your own OpenAI or Anthropic API key and pay the provider directly; others (including AltText.ai and OpptiAI Alt Text) manage the AI for you, so there's no key to configure. Bring-your-own-key can be cheaper at very high volume but adds setup. Managed tools are simpler to start with — you install, activate, and generate without touching an API dashboard.
