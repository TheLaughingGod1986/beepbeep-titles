Most AI alt text tools have the same quiet flaw: they write the description straight to your live site.

That's fine until it isn't. AI alt text is usually good — but "usually" is a problem when the description is wrong on a client's product page, or when it confidently describes a chart as "a group of people," and it's already public. Nobody reviewed it, because the tool never asked anyone to.

On accessibility work especially, that's backwards. The whole point of alt text is that a real person can rely on it. Handing that to an unreviewed model and pushing it live skips the one step that matters: someone looking at it and saying "yes, that's right."

I've started treating a review-and-approve step as non-negotiable — scan, generate, check, then save. It's slower by a few seconds per image. It's also the difference between "we added alt text" and "we added alt text we can stand behind."

How does your team check AI-generated alt text before it ships — or does it go straight to the page?
